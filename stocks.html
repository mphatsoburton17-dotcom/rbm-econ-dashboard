What is the Stock Market?

What is a share?

When a company sells "shares," it's selling small pieces of ownership in itself to raise money. If you buy a share of a company, you own a tiny slice of it — and if the company does well, your slice is usually worth more. If it does poorly, it can be worth less.

What is the Malawi Stock Exchange (MSE)?

The MSE is where shares of Malawi's publicly listed companies are bought and sold — companies like Airtel Malawi, National Bank of Malawi, FDH Bank, and Illovo Sugar. It's regulated by the Reserve Bank of Malawi's Registrar of Financial Institutions, under the Securities Act of 2010, which requires listed companies to publicly disclose accurate financial information so investors can make informed decisions.

What is the MASI?

The Malawi All Share Index (MASI) is a single number that tracks the overall performance of every company listed on the MSE. When people say "the market went up" or "the market went down," they usually mean the MASI moved. It's a quick way to see the market's overall direction without checking every company individually.

How would someone actually buy shares in Malawi?

You can't buy shares directly from the exchange — you go through a licensed stockbroker, who buys and sells on your behalf. Licensed brokers operate in Blantyre, Lilongwe, and other cities, and some now offer accounts that can be opened without visiting in person. A broker can walk you through opening an account, minimum amounts, and fees before you commit any money.

How this connects to the rest of this site: the Policy Rate and inflation figures on the Dashboard directly influence the stock market. A higher Policy Rate makes borrowing more expensive for companies and makes safer options like Treasury Bills more attractive than stocks. High inflation erodes the real value of company profits and investor returns. When you see the MASI fall during a period of high inflation and a high Policy Rate, these are usually connected, not a coincidence.
